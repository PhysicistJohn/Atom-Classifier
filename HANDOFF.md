## 1. What the system is

Atom-Classifier ships an RF **automatic modulation classifier** that runs in the browser
(TypeScript, `src/embedding/`). Seven classes: `am bluetooth cw dsss fm gsm ofdm`, over 37
sub-profiles.

Pipeline: raw complex I/Q -> **front end** (`training/preprocess.py`) -> **embedding CNN**
(38k params) -> **nearest class prototype** + an `unknown_threshold` for open-set rejection.

The front end is where most of the trouble lives. It: estimates the occupied band from a
512-point PSD, downconverts the measured centre to DC, resamples so occupied bandwidth hits a
canonical fraction (`TARGET_FRAC = 0.5`), centre-fits to a fixed `L_OUT = 1024` samples, and
RMS-normalises. It also computes 12 hand-crafted scalar features (`iq_features`, cumulants plus
envelope/instantaneous-frequency statistics) that are fed to the network alongside the waveform.

**The shipped model scores 0.890 closed-set and is the bar.** Nothing in this work has beaten it.

Training corpus is synthetic, generated by `tools/generate-signallab-iq-corpus.ts` from
Atom-SignalLab's synthesiser, with a nuisance model over ~20 capture parameters (SNR, centre
offset, clock error, multipath, IQ imbalance, PA saturation, ADC bits, sample rate 1 MHz to
200 MHz, centre frequency 70 MHz to 10 GHz).

test is: freeze the network, attach a small head for a new quantity, train only that head.

## 4. What is solidly established

**4.1 Capture length is an unswept nuisance and it dominates everything.**
The generator says so itself at `tools/generate-signallab-iq-corpus.ts:111`:
`captureLengthSamples: 'FIXED at SAMPLE_COUNT -- not yet swept; known gap'`. Every other nuisance
was randomised. This one was moved from 4096 to 16384, which is a distribution shift with
coverage on neither side.

Mechanism: the front end resamples to a canonical bandwidth then centre-fits to 1024, so the
**fill fraction** (how much of the 1024-point input carries signal) is linear in raw capture
length. For a narrowband emitter whose measured bandwidth is floor-limited near 6/512 by the
Hann-512 mainlobe, fill is a pure function of capture length: 0.094 at N=4096, 0.375 at N=16384.
Below roughly 0.2 fill, the conv stack sees mostly zero padding and the 12 scalar features read
out 1/fill rather than modulation.

This is a property of the **data geometry, not any architecture**. A plain ridge regression on
front-end output reproduces the same lock. No model behind that front end can fix it.

**4.2 The reconstruction target is partly corrupt.**
On rows at SNR >= 36 dB, where the impaired capture should be nearly identical to its clean pair
member, coherence is only 0.388. Cause: `estimate_band` works from a 512-point PSD, so centre
quantisation is 1.95e-3 cycles/sample while a 16384-sample window tolerates 6.1e-5. That is
**32x too coarse**. Residual offset median 1.05e-4, about 1.7 phase rotations across the window.
One fine derotation lifts 0.388 to 0.644.

Caveat held honestly: the corpus applies multipath, IQ imbalance and PA saturation independently
of SNR, so part of the high-SNR deficit is legitimate equalisation work. The spurious part is
specifically the residual-CFO component.

**4.3 Absolute power cannot be estimated after the front end.**
`preprocess.py:144` divides by RMS. A 1e8 range of input gain produces byte-identical output. So
`rxPowerDbm` is not a valid transfer target; a head trained on it would fit the dataset marginal
and look like it worked. **Use it as a negative control.** `snrDb` is a ratio and survives.

**4.4 Only the bottleneck tap shows evidence of learning.**
`unet_transfer.py:211`: the trunk takes 268 inputs, being 256 pooled bottleneck plus the 12
hand-crafted features concatenated directly. So `trunk` and `embed` **contain** those features.
Their higher absolute scores are largely those numbers passing through. Scored against a matched
random-init control instead of on absolute value:

| tap | trials beating random-init with separated CIs (of 6) |
|---|---|
| `bott` | **4** (class7, profile37) |
| `trunk` | 0 |
| `embed` | 0, and below random in t5 and t6 |

Run `attach_point.py` to reproduce. No retraining needed, it recomputes from saved probe reports.

## 5. The measurement traps. Read this section.

Six wrong conclusions in one session, all from the same root cause: **reading an absolute number,
forming a story, and not checking it against a control until something forced the issue.**

**5.1 A hard-coded constant smuggled the training distribution into the "independent" probe.**
`canonical_probe.py` originally fixed N=4096, copied from `src/embedding/embedding-classifier.
test.ts:39`, which is the *old* corpus's SAMPLE_COUNT. So a model trained at 16384 geometry was
scored with 4096 probes and the mismatch was reported as "representational collapse". It was
fill-lock. **Sweep every constant an OOD probe hard-codes.** The probe now sweeps length and
gates on the worst, never the best.

**5.2 The verdict compared the bottleneck against a baseline the other taps get for free.**
`latent_transfer.py` failed the bottleneck for not beating the 12 hand-crafted features. But
`trunk` and `embed` receive those features as direct input (§4.4). The bottleneck was the only
tap asked to earn it, and the only one marked down. **This is why "transfers: False" is withdrawn
and needs recomputing with a fair rule.**

**5.3 A category error about U-Net structure.**
"The bottleneck is nearly empty, therefore the architecture cannot support a reusable summary."
Wrong. A U-Net's skip connections are the design; its middle is not an autoencoder bottleneck and
nothing forces it to be a complete summary. Near-empty is close to intended behaviour. The user
caught this. It is not evidence against the architecture.

**5.4 Ranking on a statistic that floors.**
Trials were ranked on `worst_bal`, the minimum balanced accuracy over the length sweep. N=4096
sits below the fill range cropping can cover, so four of five trials floor at 0.000 there. That
made the **best** length response in the campaign (t3, base+crop: 0.000/0.433/1.000/1.000) look
identical to the worst, and it was deprioritised on that basis. Rank on the whole curve.

**5.5 Flat at chance is not invariance.**
The z-plane front-end test (§7) initially reported the new transform as the winner because its
length response was flat. It was flat because it sat at chance. A dead readout is perfectly flat.
Any invariance test **must** carry a validity floor: the matched-condition score has to clear a
bar or the spread is not reportable. `zplane_front.py` now does this.

**5.6 An absent measurement that still writes a file looks exactly like a finished one.**
`tap_fn_for` dispatched on `isinstance` and silently fell through for `TransferUNet`, so every
trial reported `{"transfers": null, "why": "no tap 'bott'"}` while still writing a 17 KB report.
Check that your primary measurement actually produced a value, not just a file.

**An independent audit of the overnight report raised 46 challenges and upheld 29, eight rated as
overturning.** The full text is in the workflow output referenced in §8. It has not yet been
folded into `OVERNIGHT_2026-07-27.md`, so **treat that document as unreliable** and prefer this
one.

## 7. The z-plane invariant front end (the live proposal)

User's idea, and the most promising direction because it attacks §4.1 at the root rather than
augmenting around it:

> The U-Net can be the transformed space in a graph neural operator, to keep inputs and outputs
> invariant. ... The graph would just give us an invariance for sampling. We could do a z-plane
> invariant Fourier transform into the input of the U-Net so it always saw an invariant space.
> We could keep the size tiny too.

Two mechanisms, and they are separate claims:
- **Scale / sample rate.** Scaling in frequency is translation in log-frequency, and a conv net is
  already translation-equivariant. So the network gets rate invariance from its own structure.
- **Capture length.** A spectral representation with a fixed bin count has the same shape whatever
  the input length. Longer captures buy a better estimate, not a differently-shaped input. The
  fill pathology ceases to exist.

"Keep it tiny" follows rather than compromises: the 38k CNN reaches 0.890 precisely because the
front end already supplies frequency/scale/power normalisation. Invariance the front end supplies
is capacity the model does not spend.

**State: first implementation (`zplane_front.py`) FAILS its own validity guard.**

| front end | 2048 | 4096 | 8192 | 16384 | spread | valid? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| current | 0.353 | 0.444 | 0.507 | 0.602 | 0.249 | yes |
| z-plane warp | 0.165 | 0.243 | 0.186 | 0.228 | 0.078 | **NO** |

Flat, and flat at chance. Suspected causes, not separated:
1. No downconversion to measured centre before warping, so centre offset and scale are two
   confounded translations on the log axis.
2. Real and imaginary parts interpolated separately across a sparse log axis. The complex spectrum
   oscillates rapidly under time offset, so interpolating between distant bins aliases phase away,
   and phase is why the representation is complex.
3. `F_CORE` and `DECADES` were guessed, not fitted to the corpus bandwidth distribution.

**And a limit of the test itself.** Log-frequency converts scaling to translation; the payoff is
conv translation-equivariance. A **ridge readout is not translation-equivariant**, so a linear
probe may be structurally unable to demonstrate this transform's benefit even when the transform
is correct. A small conv probe is probably required, which costs training.

Open questions for the user, not yet answered:
- Does this live in Atom-Classifier or the neural repo? Same z-plane machinery, and there is an
  own-repo intent around that work.
- Absorb sample rate as well as capture length? Length brittleness is measured. Rate invariance is
  a design goal with no failing measurement behind it. Building both at once makes attribution
  impossible.

## 9. Footguns. These destroy data or the shipped model.

1. **Corpus generation is destructive only after a successful complete run, but still deliberate.**
   `tools/generate-signallab-iq-corpus.ts` is now import-safe, writes PID-scoped temporary files,
   refuses a dirty SignalLab checkout, and requires `SIGNAL_LAB_SOURCE_COMMIT` before replacing
   the default corpus. Use `OUTPUT_DIR` for smoke generation. A successful default run atomically
   renames its completed outputs over `corpus.f32`, `corpus_clean.f32`, and `corpus.json`; those
   large outputs remain outside git (`.gitignore:18`).
2. **Never run `training/train_signallab.py` directly.** `ASSET_DIR` (line 37) points at
   `src/embedding/assets`, so it overwrites the **shipped model** in place with no backup.
3. **MPS cannot backward through a boolean-masked complex tensor.** Mask the real-valued loss
   instead. This killed every trial in 3 seconds until fixed (`eeda0ef`).
4. Corpora on disk: `training/artifacts/signallab-corpus` (16384/item, current) and
   `signallab-corpus-backup-20260723` (4096/item, behind the shipped assets).
5. Shipped assets snapshot: `training/artifacts/_shipped-assets-snapshot-20260726/`. Live weights
   sha256 starts `4c566a17`. Suite green at 117/117. **Nothing here has been shipped.**
